    IBEROPS (original)

    Status: Stable | Version: v1.0.0 (2026-08-28)

    General description

    • Description: Individual-tree growth model for Pinus sylvestris in the Central and Iberian Mountain Ranges (Spain)
    • Target Species: Pinus sylvestris (code: 21)
    • Application Region: Iberian Mountain Range and Central System (Ávila, Burgos, Segovia, and Soria; Spain)

    Technical characteristics

    • Model Level: Individual tree
    • Allows Projection: Yes
    • Projection Time Step: 5 years
    • Notes: This forest growth model preserves the original growth equations, while a calibrated version should work better for SNFI datasets.

    To view the step-by-step sequence of initialization, projection, and harvest, see the individual-tree model calculation flow diagram.

    Required variables

    To run this model in SIMANFOR, the initial inventory must include the following minimum data:

    Plot level (Plots sheet)

    • Inventory and plot IDs
    • age
    • dominant height
    • basal area

    Tree level (Trees sheet)

    • Inventory, plot and tree IDs
    • species
    • expansion factor
    • dbh

    Modules and equations

    Breakdown of simulated biological processes and their methodological references:

    • Site index / Stand productivity: Rojo & Montero (1996)
    • Growth: Lizarralde (2008)
    • Survival and/or mortality: Bravo-Oviedo et al. (2006)
    • Ingrowth (trees of inventoriable size): Bravo et al. (2008)
    • Stand Density Index (SDI) reference value: del Río & Montero (2011)
    • Height-diameter relationship / height estimation: Lizarralde (2008), Vázquez-Veloso et al. (2025)
    • Crown dimensions: Lizarralde (2008)
    • Tree volume and taper: Rodríguez et al. (2015), Rodríguez (2009), MITECO (2025)
    • Biomass: Ruiz-Peinado et al. (2011)
    • Carbon content: Montero et al. (2005)
    • Diversity indexes: Omoro et al. (2010), Simpson (1949), Ulanowicz (2001), Bray & Curtis (1957)
    • Structural and size indexes: Varga et al. (2005), Moreno-Fernández & others (2025), Barbeito (2009)
