    Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

    This section gathers direct answers and practical solutions to the most common questions raised by forest managers, researchers, and students when using SIMANFOR. Questions are organized across five thematic areas to facilitate quick navigation.

    General and access questions

    Is SIMANFOR a free tool?

    Yes. SIMANFOR is free of charge for education, scientific research, technical consulting, and public or private forest management, with no licensing fees or restrictions on the number of simulations.

    Do I need to install any software on my computer?

    No. The primary access pathway runs entirely in the cloud at simanfor.es. You only need a standard web browser (Firefox, Chrome, Edge, or Safari) and an internet connection. Calculations are processed on University of Valladolid servers.

    In which languages are the web platform and the simulator available?

    • Web platform (simanfor.es): The user interface allows selecting different languages for navigating menus and configuration windows.
    • Simulator engine and Excel outputs: The core biometric computation engine and result workbooks are formally verified and supported in Spanish and English.
    • Technical documentation: The official portal is maintained synchronously in both languages (ES / EN).

    How should I cite SIMANFOR in a report or scientific publication?

    The general reference paper to cite formally is:

    Bravo, F., Ordóñez, C., Vázquez-Veloso, A., and Michalakopoulos, S. (2025). SIMANFOR cloud Decision Support System: Structure, content, and applications. Ecological Modelling, 499, 110912. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2024.110912

    In addition, we recommend citing the specific growth model paper used in your simulation, whose full bibliographic references can be found in its respective technical sheet in the Models section.

    Inventories and data preparation

    Why does the simulator reject common species names?

    SIMANFOR requires identifying tree species using either their scientific Latin name (e.g., Pinus sylvestris, Quercus pyrenaica) or their official Spanish National Forest Inventory numerical code (e.g., 21 for Pinus sylvestris, 43 for Quercus pyrenaica).

    Common vernacular names vary substantially across regions, countries, and dialects (pino albar, Scots pine, pino silvestre, pino de Valsaín), which would introduce biological ambiguities into equation assignment.

    What happens if tree heights are missing from my individual-tree inventory?

    You do not need to discard the plot. In tree-by-tree inventories where height was measured only on a representative sample of trees, SIMANFOR automatically applies local or generalized height-diameter ($h/d$) submodels calibrated for each species and region, imputing the heights of the remaining stems.

    What is the expansion factor (expan) and why is it mandatory?

    The expansion factor represents the number of trees per hectare represented by each sampled tree in the inventory plot. It depends directly on the sampling design: * Fixed-radius plots ($r$): $\text{expan} = \frac{10000}{\pi \cdot r^2}$. For example, in a 15 m circular plot ($706.86\text{ m}^2$), each measured tree has an $\text{expan} \approx 14.15\text{ trees/ha}$. * Concentric NFI plots: The expansion factor varies depending on the concentric circle radius in which the tree was measured according to its diameter class (nested circular subplots with radii of 5 m, 10 m, 15 m, or 25 m).

    The simulator requires expan to accurately scale individual-tree attributes to per-hectare stand values ($N$, $G$, $V$, biomass, and carbon).

    What is the difference between the Parcelas / Plots sheet and the PiesMayores / Trees sheet?

    In SIMANFOR Excel templates: * Parcelas (or Plots) sheet: Each row defines a plot or stand with geographic identifiers and global variables (stand age, slope, site index, etc.). * PiesMayores (or Trees) sheet: Each row describes an individual tree measured in the field, specifying its species, diameter at breast height (dbh), height (h), and expansion factor (expan). * Critical rule: The identifiers ID_Inventario and ID_parcela must match exactly between both sheets so that the computation engine can link trees to their corresponding stand.

    Forest models and parameterization

    How do I choose the right forest model for my stand?

    To select the appropriate model from the SIMANFOR catalog, evaluate four biometric criteria: 1. Species composition: Is the stand pure (single species) or mixed? 2. Input data resolution: If you have tree-by-tree data, select an individual-tree model. If you only have aggregate stand metrics ($N$, $G$, $Ho$, age), choose a stand-level model. 3. Geographic scope and provenance: Many models feature regional parameterizations (e.g., coastal Galicia vs. inland Galicia vs. Central Mountain Range). 4. Input data requirements: Verify that your inventory contains all variables required by the selected model.

    You can inspect the detailed specifications of each model in the model technical sheets.

    Why shouldn't the time step (time) of a projection be modified arbitrarily?

    Each dynamic growth model was scientifically calibrated using permanent plots or repeated inventories over a specific biological time interval (typically 5- or 10-year periods).

    Modifying this interval to force annual projections or non-validated projection intervals can introduce numerical biases into mortality functions or nonlinear diameter and height growth rates. Always follow the recommended step indicated in the model technical sheet.

    Can I incorporate my own model into the simulator?

    Yes. SIMANFOR is a collaborative open project receptive to integrating new forestry equations. If you have developed or calibrated a growth model, allometric relationships, or mortality functions for species or regions not yet covered, contact the team at simanfor.forest@uva.es to explore implementation in the simulator.

    Silvicultural scenarios and interventions

    What is the practical difference between thinning from below and thinning from above?

    • Thinning from below: Trees from the dominated stratum—namely, trees with smaller diameter at breast height—are preferentially harvested. This reduces competition-induced mortality and concentrates resources on the dominant stratum without drastically modifying dominant height ($Ho$).
    • Thinning from above: Selected trees from codominant and dominant strata competing directly with target crop trees—namely, trees with larger diameter at breast height—are harvested. This promotes crown expansion in crop trees and typically provides higher immediate income from sawlog sales.

    Why doesn't the simulator allow running a harvest directly without an initial projection?

    The calculation engine must initialize all biological vigor variables, inter-tree competition metrics, and allometric coefficients from raw inventory data. The first projection step establishes model internal consistency before mathematical selection and biomass extraction rules can be evaluated.

    Why can't I find options for pruning, fertilization, or cork stripping?

    Currently, SIMANFOR only implements tree removal operations (thinnings and cleanings).

    In quantitative silviculture, implementing a technical intervention requires an empirically validated mathematical model that quantifies its direct effect on radial growth, allometry, and tree survival. As the scientific community develops and validates these functions, they will be incorporated into the platform.

    Troubleshooting and common issues

    Why does the web simulation return an empty or 0-byte ZIP file?

    In many cases, this simply occurs because the server has not yet finished calculations and the user attempts downloading the file prematurely. If this happens, it is recommended to refresh the page (F5), check the simulation status or re-launch the run, and wait until the platform generates and delivers the ZIP file with the results.

    If after waiting the file remains empty, it usually indicates that the calculation engine has detected a structural inconsistency in the inventory or scenario and aborted execution preventively. The most frequent causes are: 1. Incorrect or non-standard column headers in Excel sheets (e.g., hidden spaces or unrecognized capitalization). 2. Trees in the PiesMayores / Trees sheet whose ID_Inventario or ID_parcela identifiers do not match those in the Parcelas / Plots sheet. 3. Botanical species not supported by the selected forest model. 4. A scenario with harvest intensities attempting to extract more than 100% of standing stock.

    Review your file structure against the official templates in the Inventories section.

    Why does my scenario remain in WAITING status?

    The WAITING status indicates that your simulation is queued on the server. If several simulations are running concurrently, it may take a few minutes. If it does not advance after a reasonable period, refresh the page with F5. If it remains blocked, verify that your inventory does not contain thousands of plots exceeding web execution thresholds, or contact technical support.

    What is the difference between warnings and errors in the Diagnostico sheet?

    The SIMANFOR output workbook includes a dedicated quality control tab (Diagnostico): * Warnings: Point out advisory biometric or biological anomalies (e.g., a tree with an atypical height/diameter ratio or a plot whose density lies at the boundary of calibration data). The simulation continues successfully. * Fatal errors: Indicate severe mathematical inconsistencies preventing biological calculations (e.g., zero basal area, unsupported species, or missing mandatory data). The simulation stops to prevent erroneous output generation.
